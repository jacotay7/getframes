    A WFS camera in a closed loop¶

    An adaptive-optics loop reads its wavefront-sensor (WFS) camera once per step: a small frame, a short exposure, often a photon-starved signal, and thousands of frames per simulated second. This guide covers what changes when a getframes camera plays that role: how exposure relates to the frame period, how to turn ADU back into photons with Calibration, what noise to expect, which presets to start from, and how to keep each frame cheap.

    One frame per loop step¶

    import numpy as np
import getframes as gf

cam = gf.Camera.from_preset("andor_ocam2k").with_config(resolution=(256, 256))
cal = gf.Calibration.from_camera(cam, exposure=1e-3, n_darks=64, seed=0)

photons = np.full(cam.resolution, 0.5)  # your WFS optics: photons/pixel this frame
for step in range(100):
    frame = cam.expose(photons / 1e-3, 1e-3, seed=step, include_truth=False)
    image = cal.to_photons(frame)  # photons/pixel, ready for the centroider
    # ... slopes -> reconstructor -> DM -> next step's photons ...

    expose takes a photon rate (photons/s/pixel), so an optics model that returns photons per frame divides by the exposure. Seeding each step (seed=step, or seeds derived from your own generator) makes every loop iteration reproducible on its own.

    Exposure versus frame period¶

    getframes simulates one integration of length exposure followed by one readout:

    • Photo-signal, dark current and detector glow scale with exposure. The photon rate is integrated over it.
    • Read noise, kTC/reset noise and clock-induced charge (CIC) are per frame, independent of exposure. At kHz rates CIC usually dominates the EMCCD dark signal: the OCAM2K preset's 0.0049 e-/pixel/frame CIC is three times its 1.6 e-/pixel/s dark current integrated over 1 ms.

    The frame period is the loop's business. A frame-transfer EMCCD such as the OCAM2K integrates the next frame while it reads the last, so its exposure is the whole frame period (1/2067 s at Keck's 2067 fps). A camera with dead time, or a loop that runs slower than the camera, exposes for less than the period: pass the integration time as exposure, not the period. Readout latency, rolling-shutter timing and any delay between exposure and DM command are not part of a single frame; model them in the loop.

    From ADU to photons¶

    Calibration inverts the mean signal chain:

    electrons = L^-1[(ADU - D) * g / G] / F        (input-referred: collected in the pixel)
photons   = electrons / QE

    with D the master dark (the per-pixel mean of exposure-matched dark frames: bias pedestal, structured bias, dark current, glow and CIC), g the conversion gain in e-/ADU, G the EM or avalanche gain, L the detector nonlinearity (inverted exactly by default), F an optional unit-mean flat, and QE the quantum efficiency expose applied. It runs on NumPy or CuPy frames and keeps the camera's working precision.

    cal = gf.Calibration.from_camera(cam, exposure=1e-3, n_darks=64, seed=0)
cal.e_per_adu  # input electrons per ADU: 28 / 600 for the OCAM2K
electrons = cal.to_electrons(frame)
photons = cal.to_photons(frame)  # / config.quantum_efficiency
photons_700 = cal.to_photons(frame, wavelength_nm=700.0)  # / qe_curve(700 nm)
bad = cal.saturated(frame)  # pixels whose charge was clipped

    Camera.calibration(...) is the same as Calibration.from_camera(camera, ...). For real data, or a dark built some other way, construct gf.Calibration(config, dark, flat=...) directly.

    How accurate is it?¶

    Every stage of the forward chain is linear in expectation (Poisson shot noise, the Gamma gain stage, zero-mean read noise, round-to-nearest digitisation with at least half an ADU of read noise), so the mean of to_photons over many frames equals the incident photons. The test suite checks this for every WFS detector class with a 64x64 Shack-Hartmann spot pattern averaged over 150 frames:

    Class Presets tested Tolerance on the mean calibrated flux
    EMCCD andor_ocam2k, andor_ixon_ultra_888 0.5%
    qCMOS hamamatsu_orca_quest_2 0.5%
    sCMOS generic_scmos 0.5%
    eAPD generic_eapd, leonardo_saphira 0.5%
    eAPD first_light_imaging_cred_one, with a flat and a per-frame background from unlit pixels 1%
    CCD / CMOS generic_ccd, generic_cmos 0.5%

    The C-RED One preset carries a 69 ADU per-frame common-mode readout term, about 6 input electrons on every pixel of a frame. It is zero-mean, so it does not bias the calibration, but it dominates a frame's summed flux; a pipeline removes it from reference or unlit pixels, as the test does.

    A single frame is much noisier than that. Its total flux scatters by the shot noise times the excess-noise factor plus the read noise summed over every pixel: for a 288x288 frame with 100 photons per subaperture (about 20,000 photons in all) the per-frame scatter of the total is 1.2% for the OCAM2K, 1.0% for the ORCA-Quest 2 and 3.3% for the generic sCMOS, whose 1.6 e- read noise summed over 83,000 pixels rivals the signal. Averaged over 300 frames the same prototype recovers 1.002, 0.999 and 1.001 of the incident flux.

    Where a 1--2% calibrated-flux offset comes from

    With a mean master dark, the matching scalar QE and no saturation, the calibrated flux of expose frames has no deterministic offset; a 1--2% deficit on a handful of frames is the per-frame scatter above. Offsets that are deterministic come from the pipeline or the physics, and each has a remedy:

    • A median master dark (Camera.master_dark's default) misses the mean of a skewed dark: EM-amplified CIC, hot pixels, RTS. The calibrated flux then reads high by the missing mean, about 8% at 0.05 photons/pixel on the OCAM2K. Calibration.from_camera always averages.
    • The QE. expose applies the scalar quantum_efficiency; spectral mode applies the qe_curve. They can differ by tens of percent (the ORCA-Quest 2 scalar 0.85 is its peak; its curve gives 0.50 at 700 nm), so pass wavelength_nm= or bandpass= to to_photons for spectral frames.
    • Nonlinearity bends bright pixels low (generic_scmos: 0.67% at 2/3 of full well). Calibration inverts it unless linearize=False.
    • Saturation. Charge above the input well, the output-register ceiling (output_full_well_e / em_gain: 467 e- per pixel for the OCAM2K at gain 600) or the ADC range is lost; saturated() flags those pixels.
    • Fixed patterns. PRNU and gain maps average out over a frame but not over a few-pixel spot; pass a flat (for example cam.master_flat(..., bias=cal.dark_adu, method="mean")).

    Noise referred to the input¶

    Camera.effective_read_noise_e returns the per-pixel read noise of one ordinary frame in electrons collected in the pixel: the output-amplifier read noise (the quadrature mean of a per-pixel sCMOS map), kTC/reset noise and any avalanche-scaled term, all divided by the EM/avalanche gain:

    gf.Camera.from_preset("andor_ocam2k").effective_read_noise_e()  # 0.36 e-

    A gain stage also multiplies the shot noise of everything it amplifies by the excess noise factor F (config.gain_excess_noise_factor): sqrt(2) for an EMCCD at high gain, about 1.2--1.25 for HgCdTe eAPDs. In input electrons the per-pixel signal-to-noise of a signal S on a dark-plus-CIC level D is

    SNR = S / sqrt(F^2 (S + D) + sigma_eff^2)

    so an EMCCD buys its sub-electron read noise at the price of halving the effective QE (F^2 = 2) for shot-noise-limited pixels. At equal QE a gain-free detector with read noise sigma_1 beats an EMCCD with sigma_E once S + D > (sigma_1^2 - sigma_E^2) / (F^2 - 1): about 0.06 e- per pixel for the ORCA-Quest 2 (0.44 e-) against the OCAM2K (0.36 e-), about 3 e- for a 1.75 e- sCMOS.

    eAPD first_light_imaging_cred_one 256x320, 24 µm 0.70 2.6 e- 1.21 HgCdTe infrared WFS; PRNU 5.5% and a gain map (use a flat); 69 ADU common mode
    eAPD leonardo_saphira 256x320, 24 µm 0.70 1.16 e- 1.25 Avalanche gain 50
    CCD scimeasure_little_joe_ccd39 80x80, 24 µm 0.905 4.5 e- 1.00 The classic CCD39 WFS camera

    Effective read noise is Camera.effective_read_noise_e() for the preset as shipped (for a per-pixel sCMOS map it exceeds the nominal read_noise_e, which is the mean rather than the quadrature mean of the map).

    The Nüvü EMCCD presets saturate at one electron

    nuvu_hnu_240 and nuvu_hnu_128_omega set full_well_e to 500 and 800 e- and no output_full_well_e, so that value also caps the amplified charge at the digitiser. At EM gain 1000 a pixel holding a single electron saturates, and calibrated fluxes come out a few percent of the truth. Until the presets carry a sourced output-register capacity, set one yourself, e.g. with_config(output_full_well_e=...), or use andor_ocam2k, which models the same CCD220 with Keck-measured input and output wells. Gains and read modes are preset values; override them with

    with_config(em_gain=..., read_noise_e=...) to explore a camera's operating range (the input-referred noise of an EMCCD scales as read_noise_e / em_gain).

    Keeping frames cheap¶

    A closed-loop study can spend most of its time in expose. On the CPU a frame is bound by NumPy's Poisson, Gamma and Gaussian samplers, so:

    • Simulate the WFS format, not the whole sensor. with_config(resolution=(256, 256)) builds a 256x256 detector. A detector roi is different: it simulates the full sensor (amplifier geometry, fixed patterns) and then crops, which is right for sensor studies but costs a full-sensor frame each step.
    • Shrink the config before building the camera for a large-format preset: gf.Camera(gf.load_preset("hamamatsu_orca_quest_2").replace(resolution=(256, 256))). Camera.from_preset(...).with_config(...) first builds the full sensor's fixed-pattern maps (4096x2304 for the ORCA-Quest 2), which costs seconds and, on a GPU, hundreds of megabytes.
    • include_truth=False skips the noise-free truth arrays.
    • Reuse a DetectorWorkspace and an out frame (expose(..., workspace=ws, out=buf)) to avoid per-frame allocations: 13% for the OCAM2K frames below, little for the other presets.
    • device="gpu" runs the chain as two fused CUDA kernels; see the GPU guide for frames/s on several GPUs.

    On the CPU a 256x256 frame costs about 4--7 ms (an Intel i7-10700 and an Ampere Neoverse-N1 core), about 85% of it in NumPy's Poisson, Gamma and Gaussian samplers. Their draws come from one sequential stream, and a seeded frame must stay reproducible, so they cannot be swapped or threaded. Since 2.6.0 the Gamma gain stage samples only the pixels that hold charge, which NumPy's sampler would otherwise visit one by one to return zero; the draws, their order and the generator state are unchanged, so seeded frames are bit-identical to 2.5.0. Measured with benchmarks/bench_small_frames.py (1 ms, seeded, include_truth=False, one Neoverse-N1 core):

    Preset Photons/subaperture 2.5.0 (ms) 2.6.0 (ms) 2.6.0 + workspace/out (ms)
    andor_ocam2k 100 6.49 5.84 5.05
    andor_ocam2k 2000 7.36 6.87 6.07
    andor_ixon_ultra_888 100 5.72 5.04 5.04
    first_light_imaging_cred_one 100 7.19 6.81 6.82
    hamamatsu_orca_quest_2 100 3.88 4.03 4.02
    generic_scmos 100 4.15 4.28 4.29

    EMCCD and eAPD frames gain 5--14%; presets without a gain stage run the same code as before (their differences are run-to-run drift). The full tables, an in-process A/B and the raw data are in benchmarks/small-frame-results.md. For kHz-rate studies that need much more, use the GPU.
